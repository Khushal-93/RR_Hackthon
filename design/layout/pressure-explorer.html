Pressure Explorer: Match State Volatility

Interactive analysis of win probability swings, leverage index distributions, and situational heatmaps.

What is Pressure? The Leverage Index (LI) Formulation

Leverage Index (LI) measures how much a single delivery can swing the match outcome relative to an average ball. A ball with LI = 2.0 has twice the outcome-determining impact of a standard delivery.

To avoid penalizing Innings 1 players (whose absolute win probabilities swing less than chasing teams facing a ticking run-rate clock), pressure is standardized strictly within each innings. High Pressure is defined as the top third (≥ 66.7th percentile) within that innings:

Innings 1 High Pressure: LI ≥ 1.258 Innings 2 High Pressure: LI ≥ 1.246
Why Within-Innings Standardization?
Without separate normalization, Innings 2 death overs crowd out all Innings 1 play, falsely implying that setting a first-innings foundation carries zero match leverage.

Tactical Takeaway: Two Distinct Types of Pressure in T20 Cricket
The dual heatmaps confirm that "pressure" in T20 cricket is not a single uniform phenomenon:
  • Innings 1 Pressure = Setting Leverage: Dominated by early overs (1–6) and top-order batters (orders 1–3) navigating new-ball swing while setting an unanchored par score.
  • Innings 2 Pressure = Chasing Leverage: Dominated by death overs (16–20) and middle/lower-order finishers and death bowlers executing under escalating run-rate clocks.
